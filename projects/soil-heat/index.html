How fast heat soaks into the ground

In 2023 I scraped 2.7 million readings from India's automatic weather stations. In 2026 I went back, cleaned out the broken sensors, and used the daily temperature wave to measure the soil's thermal properties at each station. Then I recomputed, line by line, how much infrared heat the sky sends back down.

The ground damps the daily swing, much as conduction predicts

Sunlight heats the surface every day and the soil passes that wave downward. Heat conduction says the swing should shrink exponentially with depth and arrive later the deeper you go. Fitting both gives two independent estimates of the same property, the soil's thermal diffusivity.

59 stations where the two estimates agree within 1.5×. Each grey line is one station; the orange curve is the conduction model at their median damping depth. Below 30 cm the swing approaches the sensors' 0.1 °C resolution, so the few deep points sit above the curve. Two stations whose swing grew with depth, a sign of a faulty shallow sensor, are left out.

The data, and what was wrong with it

Field sensors fail quietly. Before any model, the job was finding the readings that could not be real.

Source

India Meteorological Department automatic weather stations, scraped state by state with Selenium: 2.7 million readings from 900 stations, 29 September to 28 December 2023.

Soil

194 stations also measure soil temperature at 10, 30, 70 and 100 cm, alongside air temperature, humidity, solar radiation and soil moisture.

Stuck sensors

A reading that repeats exactly for longer than that depth can plausibly stay flat: 6 hours at 10 cm, 12 at 30 cm, 48 at 70 and 100 cm. This removed 8% of hours at 10 cm and 11% at 30 cm.

Impossible heat

Soil that stays more than 12 °C hotter than the air above it, on average, all autumn. Nine stations failed this at 10 cm, some reporting soil averaging over 40 °C between October and December. They were dropped.

A lesson

My first cleaning rule used one threshold for every depth. Deep soil changes so slowly that flat stretches at 100 cm are normal, and the rule deleted three quarters of the deep readings. The diffusivity it produced was closer to rock than soil, which is how I caught it.

The strongest single input in the linear model is the average air temperature over the past 24 hours, well ahead of the current reading. The soil responds to the day, not the hour, which is the damping in the chart above showing up in a regression.

An R² of 0.47 on unseen stations also says what the weather inputs cannot explain. The rest is the site itself: soil type, moisture history, shading and sensor placement. That is what a next version would need.

The other half: heat coming down from the sky

The ground also gains heat from the atmosphere, which glows in the infrared. Building energy models estimate that glow with formulas based on surface humidity alone. I recomputed it from first principles for Chennai and tested those formulas against it.

Infrared radiation reaching the ground on Chennai's driest and most humid clear-sky soundings, computed line by line from HITRAN water vapour and CO₂ lines plus the water vapour continuum. Humid air fills in the window where a dry sky lets the ground radiate to space. The few narrow notches are a modelling limit: line wings are truncated and the continuum is only included from 350 to 1400 cm⁻¹, which leaves at most about 4 W/m², around 1%, out of each total.

Soundings

6,750 radiosonde launches from Chennai, 2000 to 2009 (the station's record ends in 2009). 2,962 were complete and clear-sky; 396 were sampled evenly across months.

Physics

Water vapour and CO₂ absorption line by line, a water vapour continuum across the thermal window, and a layer-by-layer flux calculation from the surface to 10 hPa. Clear sky means relative humidity under 95% up to 500 hPa.

Result

Mean sky emissivity 0.84 (range 0.65 to 0.94), mean downwelling 392 W/m². About 0.80 in the dry January to March months and 0.86 in the humid months.

The published formulas explain almost none of the variation at Chennai, and refitting them only helps a little. What works is total water vapour in the column: error drops to 0.031, about 38% below the published Brunt formula. In humid tropical air the radiation reaching the ground comes from the lowest few kilometres, and humidity measured at the surface says little about how much water sits above it.
